# PROP-PR-6 v1.0 — Shared Atlas & Onboarding Case Studies

**Status:** Proposal  
**Prepared for:** Joshua Asoh portfolio  
**Scope:** Content and presentation proposal only; no website implementation is included.

## Summary

Present Atlas and onboarding as related work from the same Hartford platform team, while keeping the stories distinct and making Joshua's individual contribution explicit. A shared Atlas case study can explain the team context and the Atlas effort; a separate onboarding case study can focus on the developer onboarding problem, the workflows and enablement Joshua contributed, and only outcomes that are independently verified.

The portfolio and the public reference currently show different platform scale figures: Joshua's materials say **3,000+ engineers**, while the reference page says **4,000+**. Do not select, combine, or repeat either figure as a settled current number until the owner confirms the source, measurement date, and intended definition.

## Proposed case studies

### 1. Atlas: shared team case study

**Purpose:** Explain what Atlas is, who it serves, and how Atlas Studio fits into the team's broader developer-platform work without presenting shared work as an individual project.

**Suggested narrative:**

1. **Team context:** Introduce the Hartford developer platform and Atlas at a high level. Add a verified audience-size figure only after owner review and date-stamp it.
2. **Problem and users:** Describe the developer need Atlas addresses, using approved, non-sensitive language.
3. **Shared approach:** Explain the team-level Atlas experience and how its parts fit together. Confirm names, boundaries, and approved details with the team before publication.
4. **Joshua's contribution:** Identify the specific Atlas Studio work supported by the portfolio: an AI-enabled tool that turns written guide content into narrated walkthrough videos. Verify his role, ownership boundaries, and what shipped before using stronger verbs or implementation detail.
5. **Evidence and outcome:** Include only approved, attributable evidence. Separate team outcomes from Joshua's direct deliverables and link each metric to its source and measurement period.

**Visual direction:** A simple, approved diagram showing the relationship between the developer platform, Atlas, and Atlas Studio; then a short, sanitized walkthrough or representative artifact if sharing is permitted. Do not expose internal interfaces, source content, system names, or architecture details without approval.

### 2. Developer onboarding: Joshua's contribution and team outcomes

**Purpose:** Tell a distinct story about reducing friction for new developers, rather than treating onboarding as synonymous with Atlas or implying one tool delivered every onboarding improvement.

**Suggested narrative:**

1. **Need:** Describe the onboarding challenge and intended audience after confirming what can be shared.
2. **Team context:** Explain the team's onboarding environment and existing processes at a high level.
3. **Joshua's contributions:** Validate and describe his work on Harness onboarding workflows and project provisioning, Atlas Studio's guide-to-walkthrough capability, and technical enablement for associates, interns, and engineers. Include only items relevant to the specific story and distinguish direct ownership from collaboration.
4. **Outcome:** Report a measured result only if a source, baseline, timeframe, and attribution are confirmed. If the team's public “40+ days to hours” claim is considered, verify it independently with the metric owner and do not imply Joshua alone achieved it.
5. **What changed:** Close with a concise, verified description of the resulting developer experience and the team's next steps, if appropriate.

**Visual direction:** A high-level onboarding journey (for example, entry point → approved setup/support steps → ready-to-contribute milestone). Label it as a conceptual or verified process map, as applicable; do not imply every step is automated or delivered by Atlas Studio.

## Content and layout guidance

- Keep the case studies separate, with reciprocal links only if the team confirms that Atlas and the onboarding story are meaningfully connected.
- Use a consistent case-study structure: context, problem, approach, Joshua's role, outcome/evidence, and reflection.
- Put team context before individual contribution, and visually distinguish the two. Use explicit labels such as **Team context**, **My contribution**, and **Team outcome**.
- Prefer concrete artifacts and clear diagrams over unsupported superlatives. Use sanitized screenshots or diagrams only after permission and content review.
- Avoid copying Anthony's wording or layout. Write original copy from verified facts and use the public page only as a signal of shared context and a prompt for metric reconciliation.
- Avoid confidential architecture, internal documentation, customer information, and unapproved product details.
- If an outcome cannot be verified, describe the deliverable and its intended purpose without implying measured impact.
